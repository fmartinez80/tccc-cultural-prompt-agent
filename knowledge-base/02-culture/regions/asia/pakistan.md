# Pakistan

## FILE ROLE & METHOD

This file is Pakistan's country file: a single national staging brief
with six labeled internal zones. One TCCC hero beverage per scene (named
by the brief — see HERO PRODUCT SLOT), staged against real Pakistani
dishes, vessels and settings, with the full drinks landscape — doodh
patti chai, kahwa, lassi, Rooh Afza, and the alcohol prohibition —
documented as context even where a drink is never itself staged.

**Scope.** Lunch, dinner, snacks, street food and the Ramadan iftar
table are in scope (project default plus iftar, which the Pakistan brief
named). **Breakfast (nashta) — halwa puri, nihari as a dawn dish, siri
paye, paratha — sits in an OPTIONAL MORNING MODULE, off by default**,
the same pattern as `india.md` and `brazil.md`. Nihari and paye are also
eaten at lunch and late at night, so their main entries stay in the
catalog. Both are scope calls **flagged for Fernando's confirmation**
(§1.2; log in `DECISIONS.md`). No beverage other than the hero TCCC
product is staged; the others are documented in ICONIC BEVERAGES.

### Hard staging rules specific to Pakistan (read before any scene)

1. **Halal table: no pork, ever, in any form.** Pakistan is an Islamic
   republic with a ~96% Muslim population; pork is effectively absent
   from ordinary food life [MEDIUM — uncontested; not independently
   re-checked this pass]. No ham, bacon, pork sausage, pork ribs,
   salami-looking cold cuts. **Beef is eaten freely** (unlike much of
   India — see `india.md` hard rules): beef nihari, beef haleem, beef
   seekh and chapli kebab are normal. Never let a model render kebab or
   tikka as pink ham-like meat. [EDITORIAL rule on uncontested fact]
2. **No alcohol staged, ever — and no bar or "permit room" setting.**
   Pakistan has had **total alcohol prohibition for Muslims since 1977**
   (Z. A. Bhutto); non-Muslims and foreigners need a government permit
   to buy at designated outlets, mainly upscale hotels; Murree Brewery
   (est. 1860) operates but may not advertise alcohol domestically
   [HIGH — Express Tribune, Dawn, NPR, The World (PRX) agree]. Alcohol is
   therefore documented in ICONIC BEVERAGES only as the reason it is
   absent. Never a TCCC product as a mixer.
3. **Ramadan, iftar and the two Eids are religious occasions.** No
   eating or drinking scene is set in Ramadan daylight. **At iftar the
   hero product is never staged as the first thing that breaks the fast**
   — dates (and, off-frame, water) hold that role; the hero stands
   untouched in the midground of a full, not-yet-started table (see
   FESTIVALS). **Never stage the Eid ul-Adha sacrifice**, tethered goats
   or cattle in streets, carcasses, blood or piles of raw meat.
   [EDITORIAL — flagged for Fernando]
4. **Chai, lassi and Rooh Afza are not staged beside the hero.** A model
   will add a cup of milky chai to every dhaba scene and a glass of pink
   Rooh Afza to every iftar unless told not to. Exclude them by name.
5. **Eating by hand is right-handed and bread-led**; staging shows food
   resting on the surface, never in a hand (project rule). No legible
   text anywhere; no drinks other than the hero.
6. **Mosques, prayer mats, Qur'an and Arabic calligraphy**: never in
   hero position, never beside the product; a distant blurred minaret on
   a skyline at most. Calligraphy panels on a wall are legible text —
   keep them out or fully blurred. [EDITORIAL]

### Structural decision: one file, six zones (recommendation — reviewer has final say)

The `country-file-schema.md` §1.1 swap test, applied with the evidence
gathered this pass:

- **A national core travels.** Daal chawal, salan with roti, chicken
  karahi, biryani as a category, seekh kebab and chicken tikka, samosa,
  pakora, chana chaat, fruit chaat, sheer khurma, tandoori roti and naan
  read correctly anywhere in Pakistan [MEDIUM — national reach of each
  is uncontested; mostly not independently re-checked].
- **Several dishes are form-changing by region** (§4.2) and would look
  wrong transplanted: **karahi** (Lahori — yoghurt and whole spices,
  thick red masala — vs. Peshawari namak mandi/charsi — meat, tomato,
  green chilli and the meat's own fat, almost no spice) [MEDIUM-HIGH —
  several Pakistani food sources agree, tier 2–4]; **biryani**
  (Karachi/Sindhi — tomato-heavy masala, **whole potatoes and aloo
  bukhara plums** — vs. India's Hyderabadi, which uses neither tomato
  nor potato) [MEDIUM-HIGH]; **pulao** (Peshawari/Kabuli — stock-cooked
  rice topped with sweet fried carrot shreds and raisins — vs. the
  Punjabi yakhni pulao) [HIGH — Wikipedia, Peshawar guides]; **sajji**
  (Balochistan, whole salted lamb or chicken roasted on an upright
  skewer by a fire) [HIGH].
- **Environment does not travel**: a brick-and-plaster Lahore house with
  a flat roof, a Karachi apartment block by the sea, a Peshawar old-city
  bazaar, a Quetta mud-and-stone compound under bare mountains, and a
  Hunza stone house among apricot trees look nothing alike.

**Recommendation: one national file, six zones**, handled as dish-variant
and environment deltas — the same call as Türkiye (seven zones) and
Mexico (seven), not a US-style index-plus-regional-files split. No
spinout is triggered; **zone 3 (Karachi & Sindh)** is the strongest
candidate if Karachi briefs dominate, because Karachi's apartment
register, Urdu-speaking Muhajir food culture (nihari, haleem, bun kebab,
Karachi biryani) and coastal light differ most from the Punjabi default.
**Recommendation for the reviewer, not a decision** (§7).

### Default when no zone is named

Fall back to **zone 1 (Lahore) at the everyday register**: a Lahore
house's dining area or a family seated around a dastarkhwan, or a busy
neighbourhood karahi/tikka restaurant or dhaba. Punjab holds about
**19.8 million of Pakistan's 38.3 million households (~52%)** [HIGH —
2023 Census, PBS via search], and Lahore is the country's most
food-identified city. [EDITORIAL fallback — not a sourced "most typical
Pakistan" claim]

## METHOD NOTE (read first)

**Build method, this pass.** Drafted directly from model knowledge and
checked with 40 WebSearch queries, prioritising what a scene visibly
depends on: pack formats, housing, meal times, festival dates, the
form-changing dishes (karahi, biryani, pulao, sajji, chapli kebab),
iftar, dhaba and dastarkhwan. Every WebFetch was blocked by the egress
proxy. Tags mean:

- **[HIGH] / [MEDIUM] / [LOW]** — schema §6 tags earned this pass (HIGH =
  2+ independent corroborating sources; MEDIUM = 1 credible source).
- **[… — not independently re-checked this pass]** — model knowledge that
  is uncontested general culinary or cultural knowledge but was not
  individually searched. Plausible, not verified.
- **[EDITORIAL]** — a judgment call (defaults, portion counts, surface
  shares, caricature-avoidance guidance), never a factual claim.
- **[HIGH — first-party test]** — this project's own image-generation
  findings (`coca-cola-guidelines.md` §1, `country-file-schema.md` §7.5).
  No Pakistan-specific image tests have been run.
- **"(via search)"** — the claim rests on a search-result snippet. This
  pass, that is every sourced claim.
- **Source tiers**: many Pakistani dish details rest on tier-2/3 food
  journalism (Dawn, Express Tribune, Images) and tier-4 recipe sites
  (Hinz Cooking, pakistani.recipes, Tea for Turmeric). Tier-4-only
  claims are marked.

**Cross-reference with `india.md`.** Samosa, pakora, chaat, biryani,
naan, nihari, haleem, sheer khurma and tandoor cooking are shared with
North India. This file keeps India's entries authoritative for Indian
forms and documents only the **Pakistani** form, saying where it
differs: Karachi biryani adds tomato, potato and plums where India's
default Hyderabadi has none; Pakistani menus are beef-forward where
Indian menus avoid beef; there is no vegetarian default here (India's
veg/non-veg split does not apply). Nothing here contradicts `india.md`.

**Writing principle.** Describe what the camera sees: surface, oil
separation (the "tari" — the red oil that rises on a finished karahi or
nihari), char, crust, crumb, steam, and real-world size.

**File-wide rules for every scene built from this file:**

1. **Text as atmosphere.** Shop signs (Urdu Nastaliq script is dense and
   decorative and a strong model prior on street scenes), menu boards,
   truck art lettering, packaging, cooler lids appear only as heavily
   blurred, unreadable colour. Any readable word, number or brand mark
   means reject or retouch. Blur instructions are known to fail [HIGH —
   first-party test; `country-file-schema.md` §7.5].
2. **The hero product is a TCCC beverage named by the brief** (HERO
   PRODUCT SLOT). Branding is composited in post
   (`coca-cola-guidelines.md` §1–2).
3. **No alcohol in any scene** (hard rule 2); never a TCCC product as a
   mixer.
4. **No drinks in frame other than the hero product** unless the brief
   explicitly allows a named non-alcoholic companion. Name the likely
   intruders in the negative: cups of milky chai, small glasses of green
   kahwa, tall glasses of lassi, pink Rooh Afza, steel water tumblers,
   jugs of water.
5. **Nothing held in a hand.** Bun kebab, samosa, chapli kebab in naan
   rest on a surface (`country-file-schema.md` §7.5).
6. **Halal only** (hard rule 1).

## TRUSTED CONTENT

### HERO PRODUCT SLOT

Every scene carries one TCCC hero product, **named by the brief**. This
file uses `south-africa.md`'s generalised slot; since 2026-09-27 that is
the project-wide rule (the brief dictates the SKU, never the region — see
`DECISIONS.md` and `country-file-schema.md` §5.4). **If a brief names no
product, ask for one.** Nothing below is a default.

**Template:**
> [HERO PRODUCT]: {brand and variant exactly as named on pack}, in
> {format and size}, {dominant pack colour and material cue},
> {negated lookalikes}. {Position: standing upright on the surface,
> label facing camera or turned slightly}. Pack text will be composited
> in post.

**Rules:**
- **Name the variant; negate the closest lookalike** (Coca-Cola Original
  vs. Coca-Cola Zero Sugar; Sprite vs. 7Up; Fanta vs. Mirinda). An
  unspecified "Coca-Cola can" rendered as the wrong variant in 2 of 3
  generations [HIGH — first-party test, `coca-cola-guidelines.md` §1].
- **Also negate the local competitors.** **Pepsi** has long been the
  market leader in Pakistan (older trade sources put it near 65% of
  carbonates to Coca-Cola's ~35%; Pepsi and Coca-Cola together were
  above 80–95% before 2024) [LOW-MEDIUM — student/trade documents and
  Arab News; no current share found]. Local colas **Cola Next** and
  **Pakola** (launched 14 August 1950, originally in returnable glass
  with crown caps), and **Gourmet Cola**, are the domestic lookalikes
  [HIGH for Pakola history — Wikipedia via search; MEDIUM for Gourmet
  Cola's pack range — Gourmet's own site via search]. Always state "not
  any other cola brand".
- **Pakistan is a 250 mL-can market — not 330, not 300, not 355.**
  Pakistani retailers (Carrefour, Metro, Naheed, Al-Fatah, foodpanda
  pandamart, Springs, Shams) list **Coca-Cola "Local" Can 250 mL** and
  **Coca-Cola Zero Sugar Can 250 mL**, singles and cartons of 12 [HIGH —
  six or more independent retailer listings agree (via search)]. A
  **300 mL can** appears only on one old, small retailer page (gomart.pk)
  [LOW — likely stale; do not use]. **This contradicts
  `coca-cola-guidelines.md` §4.3's "default to 330 mL for any non-US
  market"** — after Mexico (355), Brazil (350) and India (300), the
  fourth market to break it. Logged in the GAP LOG; not edited here.
- **The 250 mL can's silhouette is NOT confirmed.** Al-Fatah lists the
  Zero Sugar can as **"Coke Zero Slim Can 250 ML"** while listing the
  Original as "Coca Cola Local Can 250 ML" [MEDIUM — Al-Fatah listings
  via search]. A 250 mL can is made either as a **tall slim can**
  (roughly 13 cm tall, ~5.3 cm wide) or a **short standard-diameter
  can** (roughly 9 cm tall, ~6.6 cm wide) — the two read completely
  differently in a frame [LOW — general packaging knowledge, not
  re-checked]. No source this pass said which body the Pakistani
  Original uses. **Working anchor for this file's prompt lines: "the
  can", treated as a slim can roughly 13 cm tall and 5.3 cm wide [LOW —
  provisional, inferred from the "slim" Zero listing]; confirm from a
  real Pakistani can or CCI spec before any production run**, and use
  the plate, karahi or naan as the second scale anchor (see SCALE
  REFERENCE). Prompt lines below are written in relative terms that
  hold roughly for either body ("about the can's height").
- **Formats confirmed current in Pakistan this pass** (reference for
  whoever writes the brief — never a default):
  - **Can**: 250 mL (Original "local", Zero Sugar "slim") [HIGH].
  - **PET**: **350 mL**, **500 mL**, **1 L**, **1.5 L**, **2.25 L**
    [HIGH — Carrefour, Metro, Naheed, QnE, foodpanda listings via
    search]; a **2 L** bottle appears in one listing [LOW]. CCI Pakistan
    has launched bottles in **100% recycled PET** (the 2.25 L cited)
    [MEDIUM — CCI news page title and snippet via search].
  - **Glass**: **not confirmed current.** A historical 8 oz (~237 mL)
    returnable Pakistani Coke bottle exists as a collectible [LOW — eBay
    listing]; one grocery listing for a 250 mL glass bottle has an
    implausible price [LOW]. Do not stage a glass bottle for Pakistan
    until a current returnable-glass format is confirmed. (Contrast
    India and Türkiye, where small glass is a live dhaba/kebapçı format.)
  - **The 500 mL PET exists here** — consistent with
    `coca-cola-guidelines.md` §4.4's "500mL for a heartier individual
    meal" (unlike Türkiye's 450 mL and India's 400/600 mL).
- **Which formats fit which setting** (reference only, not a default):
  - dhaba, food street, tikka/karahi restaurant, street snack: a 250 mL
    can or a 350 mL or 500 mL PET
  - office lunch, on the go, delivery: a 250 mL can or 350/500 mL PET
  - family dinner, dastarkhwan, iftar, Eid, a BBQ night: a **1.5 L or
    2.25 L PET** in the midground with one filled plain glass per place
    (the 2.25 L is the large family bottle Pakistani shoppers buy by the
    carton of six [HIGH — listings])
  - restaurant for two or three: cans, or a 1 L PET
- **One hero product per scene** unless the brief asks for several.
- **Sensitivity flag for Fernando — consumer boycott.** CCI's own filings
  reported Pakistan **sparkling volume down 22.9% in July–September 2024**
  year on year after Gaza-related boycott calls, while local colas (Cola
  Next, Pakola) rose to about **12% of the soft-drink category from
  ~2.5%** [HIGH — Profit/Pakistan Today, ProPakistani, Mettis, Al Arabiya
  report the same CCI figures]. This does not change any staging fact
  but bears on how heavily religious-occasion scenes (iftar, Eid) should
  be used for TCCC in Pakistan. **Decision for Fernando / TCCC Pakistan,
  not this file.**

### ICONIC BEVERAGES (documented context — staging rules follow)

This section records the real Pakistani drinks landscape and restricts
only what is staged — the same design `south-africa.md` uses.

**Non-alcoholic context (never beside the hero; staged only if a brief
allows a named companion):**

- **Chai — doodh patti.** Black tea dust or granules brewed directly in
  milk with sugar, a deep tan-beige; the default home and dhaba drink.
  **Karak chai** is a stronger, longer-brewed, cardamom version popular
  at Karachi roadside stalls [MEDIUM-HIGH — Google Arts & Culture (SOCH),
  Halal Kitchen Atlas, House of Pakistan agree]. Served in small cups
  with saucers at home and in thick small glass cups or ceramic cups at
  dhabas [LOW — not re-checked]. **The single most likely intruder —
  negate it by name.**
- **Kahwa** — green tea, often with cardamom, sometimes saffron and
  almonds; predominant in **Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Balochistan and the
  north**, served after heavy meals in small handle-less cups or small
  glasses from a metal pot [HIGH — Wikipedia (Kahwah) and two tea-culture
  sources via search]. Negate in every Peshawar scene.
- **Kashmiri chai (noon chai, "pink tea")** — green tea, milk, salt or
  sugar and a pinch of baking soda, pink; winter gatherings and weddings,
  topped with crushed pistachio [HIGH — Wikipedia (Noon chai), two
  recipe/tea sources].
- **Lassi** — sweet or salted yoghurt drink, Punjab; tall steel or glass
  tumblers, often with a layer of malai (cream) [MEDIUM — not
  independently re-checked]. Negate at halwa puri and dhaba scenes.
- **Rooh Afza** — bright pink-red rose syrup diluted with water or milk;
  **the iftar drink** in Pakistan, with dates [HIGH — several iftar
  guides agree]. **The second most likely intruder — negate it in every
  iftar scene.**
- **Sugarcane juice (ganne ka ras), sattu, falooda, sherbet-e-sandal** —
  street and summer drinks [LOW — not re-checked].

**Alcohol context (never staged; see hard rule 2):**

- **Prohibition for Muslims since 1977**; permit-based purchase for
  non-Muslims and foreigners at designated outlets, mainly upscale
  hotels; **Murree Brewery** (est. 1860, Rawalpindi) is the long-standing
  licensed brewer, barred from advertising alcohol domestically, and
  sells non-alcoholic malt drinks and juices in the open market [HIGH —
  Express Tribune, Dawn, NPR (2025–26), The World]. There is no drinking
  culture to depict in mainstream food scenes; the only rule this
  creates is negative: **no bottle, glass or bar cue that reads as
  alcohol**, and never a TCCC product as a mixer.

### GENERAL NORMS

**Meal pattern — who eats when.**

- **Lunch ~13:00–14:00, after the midday (Zuhr) prayer**; **dinner late,
  ~20:30–21:30, rarely before 20:00, sometimes 22:00**; **dinner is the
  main family meal**, eaten together [MEDIUM — Remitly's meal-time guide
  (two regional versions) and factsanddetails agree; tier 3, no survey
  found].
- **§5.2 contrast**: like Türkiye, the family meal is dinner (not the
  midday meal of Spain and Mexico), but **roughly an hour to ninety
  minutes later than Türkiye's ~19:00–20:00** — close to Spain's clock.
  A Pakistani dinner scene is fully dark at the window for most of the
  year, lit by warm interior light.
- **Lahore runs three food trades a day**: a dawn-to-11:00 breakfast trade
  (halwa puri, nihari, paye), a daytime trade, and a late-night karahi and
  chargha trade [MEDIUM — Lahore food guides via search, tier 4].

| Occasion | Typical time | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Nashta (breakfast) | ~07:00–09:00; Sunday halwa puri ~06:00–11:00 | MEDIUM (halwa puri shops open 5–6 am, sell out by 10–11 — food guides) |
| Lunch | ~13:00–14:00 | MEDIUM |
| Evening chai and snack | ~17:00–18:00 | LOW — not re-checked |
| **Dinner (main family meal)** | **~20:30–21:30** | MEDIUM |
| Iftar (Ramadan) | at sunset — in Lahore roughly 17:45–18:15 in Feb–Mar 2027 | LOW-MEDIUM — check the local Ramadan timetable for the exact day |

**Table norms:**
- **Bread leads the meal in the north and centre.** Tandoori roti and
  naan from the neighbourhood tandoor (bought hot by the stack), chapati
  (phulka) made on a tawa at home; rice dishes (biryani, pulao, daal
  chawal) are the other pole, stronger in Sindh/Karachi [MEDIUM — tandoor
  bread as a national staple is HIGH (Wikipedia via search); the
  north/south emphasis not re-checked].
- **Eating with the right hand**, tearing roti to scoop salan; spoons
  for rice and for guests; forks and knives at restaurants and in more
  westernised homes [MEDIUM — not independently re-checked; uncontested].
- **Shared dishes in the middle**, each person with a plate; salan in a
  dish or the karahi it was cooked in; roti in a cloth-lined basket or an
  insulated "hot pot" (a lidded, often patterned insulated casserole)
  [LOW-MEDIUM — hot pot not re-checked; very common household object].
- **Always on the side**: **salad** (sliced onion, tomato, cucumber,
  green chilli, lemon wedges), **raita** (yoghurt with cumin, mint or
  cucumber), **achar** (pickle), sometimes green chutney [MEDIUM — not
  independently re-checked].
- **Dastarkhwan**: a cloth spread on the floor (or on a bed, a corridor,
  a table) as the eating surface — used in many Muslim South Asian homes,
  and when guests are too many for a table; family sits cross-legged
  around it [HIGH — Wikipedia (Dastarkhān), Vittles, Inspire the Mind via
  search]. **Not every home eats on the floor every day**: a dining table
  is normal in urban middle-class homes. Use the dastarkhwan for family
  gatherings, iftar, Eid and traditional/rural homes. [EDITORIAL on
  prevalence; no figure found]
- **Water jug and steel or glass tumblers** are on most tables — exclude
  them (file-wide rule 4).

### VISUAL & PLATING NORMS

- **Palette**: brick-red and rust masalas with red oil; charcoal-black
  char; white basmati with saffron patches; the bright green of coriander
  leaves and green chillies; ginger julienne in pale gold; lemon wedges;
  the creamy white of raita; tandoor-blistered breads. [EDITORIAL]
- **Cooked-and-served-in-the-vessel**: karahi arrives in its karahi;
  biryani is spooned from a deg onto a platter; nihari and haleem are
  ladled from the pot into a plate or bowl and garnished at the counter.
  [MEDIUM]
- **Garnish is functional and abundant**: julienned ginger, sliced green
  chilli, coriander, fried onions (birista), lemon wedges — a heap, not a
  chef's sprig. [MEDIUM — nihari, haleem, karahi sources]
- **Oil separation is the doneness cue**: a finished karahi, nihari or
  qorma shows a layer of red oil at the surface. Do not render it dry and
  matte, and do not render it as a cream sauce. [MEDIUM — recipe sources]
- **Meat is cooked through** — no pink kebab, no rare steak. [EDITORIAL;
  uncontested]
- **Grade neutrally.** Avoid the sepia "dusty Orient" haze image models
  attach to Pakistan. Lahore winter light is hazy and soft; summer is
  harsh white; Karachi has a sea-haze; the north is clear and blue.
  [EDITORIAL]

### ENVIRONMENT & STAGING SCENES

#### General environmental norms

- **"At home" usually means a house, not a flat — except in Karachi.**
  The 2023 Census counts **38.3 million households, average size 6.30
  persons** (down from 6.45 in 2017); **67.5% live in pakka (permanent,
  brick/concrete) structures**, 32.5% in semi-pakka or kacha (mud,
  temporary) dwellings — **87.2% pakka in cities vs 54.7% in rural
  areas**; Punjab 80.8% pakka, Sindh 57.7% (rural Sindh 61% kacha), KP
  55.6% [HIGH — PBS 2023 Census report and summaries via search]. The
  census snippets give no house-vs-flat split. Real-estate sources say
  **flats are only ~4% of Lahore's housing stock, while Karachi's stock
  is roughly half flats** (high-rise blocks of 15–20 storeys) [LOW-MEDIUM
  — Zameen blog, tier 3 with commercial interest; no census figure].
  **Default: a house in Punjab/KP/Balochistan; an apartment is the
  realistic Karachi default.** [MEDIUM for the inference]
- **Households are large and often joint** (6.3 persons on average):
  grandparents, parents and children, sometimes married sons' families
  under one roof. A "family dinner" in Pakistan is 5–8 people, not 3–4.
  [HIGH for household size; joint-family framing MEDIUM — not
  re-checked]
- **§5.2 contrast**: the opposite of Türkiye's apartment norm and closer
  to Mexico's house-dwelling majority — but with Karachi as a big
  apartment exception.
- **Interior markers (pick one or two per scene)**: terrazzo, marble-chip
  or tiled floors with a rug; a ceiling fan (nearly universal); a
  drawing room with a sofa set and a glass-topped centre table; a
  dining table with a printed cloth, sometimes covered in clear plastic;
  plastered walls in pale colours; a decorative wall clock; a
  water-dispenser or fridge in the dining area; in winter, a gas heater
  and quilts. [LOW-MEDIUM — uncontested general knowledge, not
  re-checked]
- **Exterior markers**: flat-roofed brick or plastered houses of two or
  three storeys with a boundary wall, a gate and a small front lawn or
  courtyard (Lahore, Islamabad); water tanks on the roof; laundry on the
  roof; dense mixed-use bazaars with overhanging balconies (old Lahore,
  Peshawar, Rawalpindi); Karachi apartment blocks with balconies and
  air-conditioner units; brightly painted trucks and rickshaws on the
  road (keep their lettering blurred). [LOW-MEDIUM — not re-checked]
- **The roof (chhat) and the courtyard (sehan)** are real living spaces
  — winter-sun lunches, summer evenings, Eid BBQ. [MEDIUM — not
  re-checked]
- **Gen Z lens (§5.3)**: most unmarried young adults live with parents;
  students in Lahore, Islamabad and Karachi live in hostels or shared
  flats [LOW — not re-checked]. Stage a young adult at the family table,
  on the roof with friends, or at a café/fast-casual spot (burger, zinger
  and pizza chains are very visible in cities) — neither a chaotic
  hostel room nor a sterile showroom.
- **Caricature avoidance [EDITORIAL]**:
  - **Conflict/poverty framing**: rubble, grey dust, guns, refugee camps,
    kacha mud houses as the "authentic" default. Kacha houses are real
    (a third of households) but are not the default for a food scene.
  - **Orientalist framing**: camels, deserts, turbans on every man,
    burqas on every woman, hanging lanterns and hookahs. Dress varies:
    shalwar kameez is everyday for most men and women; women may wear a
    dupatta; headscarf and burqa use varies sharply by region and family.
  - **India conflation**: Hindu iconography, vegetarian thalis on banana
    leaves, saris as default dress, Diwali lamps, bindis.
  - **Generic "curry house"**: copper balti bowls and tea lights.
  - The ordinary baseline is a tidy, lived-in middle-class house, a busy
    clean karahi restaurant, and a crowded family dastarkhwan.
- **People in frame**: modest dress, shalwar kameez; mixed-gender family
  tables at home are normal; in conservative KP/Balochistan public
  eating scenes, men-only tables are realistic and women's "family
  sections" exist in restaurants [LOW-MEDIUM — not re-checked]. Never
  stage physical contact between unrelated men and women. [EDITORIAL]

each year, and **Pakistan's dates are set by the Central Ruet-e-Hilal
(moon-sighting) Committee**, often a day after Saudi Arabia. As of
drafting (2026-10-01), the 2026 dates below are past; the 2027 dates are
the next ones a production would hit.

| Occasion | 2026 (observed) | 2027 (expected) | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ramadan (Ramzan)** | began ~19–20 Feb 2026 | **~8–9 Feb – ~9 Mar 2027** | MEDIUM — astronomical calendars (IslamicFinder et al.); moon-dependent |
| **Eid ul-Fitr** ("Meethi Eid") | **Sat 21 Mar 2026** | **~9–10 Mar 2027** | HIGH for 2026 (Pakistani press: Shawwal moon not sighted, Eid on 21 Mar); MEDIUM for 2027 |

| Independence Day | 14 Aug | 14 Aug | HIGH — uncontested |

- **Ramadan iftar.** The table is set before sunset; nobody eats until
  the maghrib call to prayer. **The fast is opened with dates (and water
  or Rooh Afza)**, then fried snacks — **pakoras, samosas** — **fruit
  chaat, chana chaat, dahi baray (dahi bhallay)**, then a heartier main
  later [HIGH — several iftar guides agree (Hinz Cooking, chaewala,
  Bazaar, Graana); the order is consistent across them]. The
  **dastarkhwan** is the classic iftar setting [MEDIUM].
  **Staging (EDITORIAL — flagged for Fernando):**
  1. Stage **the moment just before iftar**: a full, untouched table,
     dusk blue at the window, warm lamp light; no bites taken, no
     half-empty glasses.
  2. **The hero product is never the first item breaking the fast.**
     Do not place it in the foreground beside the dates, do not pour it
     at the moment of the call to prayer, do not stage an open can next
     to a single date. The dates sit in their own small bowl at the
     front; the hero stands untouched, sealed or with glasses unpoured,
     in the midground among the shared dishes, as one part of the meal
     that follows.
  3. **Keep Rooh Afza, water jugs, tumblers and chai out of frame**
     (file-wide rule 4) — this is the hardest exclusion in the file,
     because a pink Rooh Afza jug is the iconic iftar object.
  4. **No prayer mat, Qur'an, tasbih or person praying** in the frame;
     no calligraphy.
  5. This rule is this file's editorial sensitivity call, **not TCCC
     Pakistan's documented Ramadan policy**, which was not researched.
     Read together with the boycott note in HERO PRODUCT SLOT.
- **Sehri** (pre-dawn meal: paratha, eggs, yoghurt, leftover salan) —
  real, but a dark 03:00–05:00 scene; use only if briefed. [MEDIUM — not
  re-checked]
- **Eid ul-Fitr**: new clothes, mehndi on women's hands the night before
  (chand raat), family visits, **sheer khurma** served to guests in the
  morning, eidi (cash gifts) for children [MEDIUM — Pakistani and Indian
  sources agree on sheer khurma; see `india.md`; rest not re-checked].
  Stage a drawing-room scene: a bowl of sheer khurma, a plate of mithai
  and samosas or chana chaat on a glass centre table, the hero in the
  midground.
- **Eid ul-Adha**: families who can afford it sacrifice a goat, sheep,
  cow or camel and share the meat. Food: **kaleji (liver) on the first
  morning** with paratha, then **biryani at lunch** and **BBQ (seekh,
  chapli, boti) in the evening**; karahi, kebabs and slow-cooked salans
  for two or three days [MEDIUM-HIGH — several Pakistani Eid food guides

- **Weddings** (mehndi, baraat, walima): biryani or pulao, qorma, naan,

- **Winter**: Punjab's foggy, cold Dec–Jan brings saag and makki ki roti,

### A. National core and Punjab

#### Tandoori roti and naan (the bread basket — appears in most scenes)

- **Category**: Everyday — every meal in the north and centre.
- **Lineage**: Punjabi tandoor tradition, national; shared with North
  India (see `india.md`).
- **Variants (§4.6)**: **tandoori roti** (whole-wheat, unleavened, round,
  from the tandoor), **naan** (white flour, leavened, oblong teardrop),
  **khamiri roti** (yeasted, Karachi/Muhajir, with nihari), **roghni
  naan** (sesame-topped, enriched), **chapati/phulka** (thin, made on a
  tawa at home — the daily home bread). **Default when unspecified**: at
  home, chapati in a hot pot; at a restaurant or dhaba, tandoori roti and
  naan in a basket. [EDITORIAL; tandoor bread types HIGH — Wikipedia
  (Tandoor bread) via search]
- **Venue**: households buy hot tandoori roti and naan from the
  neighbourhood tandoor by the stack; Karachi tandoors sell naan at
  120 g officially, 140–180 g in practice [HIGH for neighbourhood tandoor
  purchase and weights — Dawn].
- **Vessel & scale**: a cane or plastic basket lined with cloth, or a
  hot pot; naan ~25–30 cm long, roti ~15–20 cm across [MEDIUM — Dawn
  weights; sizes tier 4].

  bubbles, soft, sometimes brushed with ghee; roti — matte light brown
